D-NE1 Bitrate Ceiling

I read your D-NE1's own manual, and it quietly changes the shape of this project. For ATRAC3plus, Sony documents exactly two transfer rates for your player: 48 and 64 kbps.

That matters because atracdenc, the encoder that passed its trial the other night, can only produce 352.8 kbps. That rate didn't exist until SonicStage 3.4 in 2006, a few years after your player was made, so the one output we already have working is the one least certain to suit it. It might still play fine, and only a test disc in your D-NE1 gets to decide that, but I wouldn't build the plan around it.

I also read atracdenc's source, and 352.8 isn't a default I can simply change. The frame size is hardcoded into the encoder, and its settings have no bitrate option at all. A 64 kbps stream uses frames of 376 bytes instead of 2,048, so giving it a real 64k mode means teaching the encoder core to fill those much smaller frames. That's doable work, but it's real work, and I'd put it on the critical path rather than in the later-polish pile.

There's a cleaner first test hiding in all this, though. Your library already holds clean 64 kbps tracks in exactly the form SonicStage burns from, so the first test disc can use one of those and skip the encoder entirely. If that disc plays, the layout and the burn-time encryption are proven, and the 64k encode is the only unknown left. If it doesn't play, we'll know the fault sits in the layout or the encryption, not in atracdenc. A 352.8 test disc couldn't tell us that, because a rejection there could mean the rate or the encoder, with no way to separate them.

The next step on my side is already unblocked: you sent the Batch 2 DLLs, so the stage-0 evidence is complete and the formatter and key-block work comes first, starting with Ghidra on CDRFormatter.dll and omgatpcd.dll. I'll keep the rate work parked right behind it, and when a test disc exists, your ears get the final word.
